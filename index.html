      How ordering works

      LEGO Pick a Brick is LEGO’s own shop for single pieces. “Buy at LEGO” downloads your list (). On Pick a Brick, click “Upload List”, choose the file, then check your bag: LEGO lists any piece it couldn’t add. Uploading a list isn’t available in every country: check Pick a Brick for yours.

      BrickLink is a marketplace of independent shops selling single pieces, including the ones LEGO doesn’t sell or has out of stock. “Buy on BrickLink” copies your list. Sign in (with a LEGO account), open Want → Upload, choose “Upload BrickLink XML format”, paste, then open the new wanted list and use “Buy All” to find shops.

      Questions

      What happens to my logo?

      A file you drop or paste never leaves your browser: reading it, designing the sign, the 3D view, the PDF and the video all happen on your device. When you type a website, its address goes to a small logo finder run for this site. It opens that page like a browser would, lists the images that look like a logo, passes the one you pick back to you, and keeps nothing: no log of what you looked up, no copies of images beyond a one-day cache.

      Why didn’t it find the right logo?

      Websites show their logo in many ways, and some draw it with code that only works inside their own page. Pick another one from the list, or download the logo yourself (press kits and “brand” pages usually have clean PNG or SVG files) and drop it here. Transparent backgrounds and flat colours give the cleanest signs.

      Panel or cut-out?

      Panel stands the logo one stud proud of a board in its background colour, like a shop sign: sturdy, and holes in letters stay readable. Cut-out builds only the logo. Parts that don’t touch anything below, like the dot on an “i”, are held by small clear bricks, and the site tells you when it adds them.

      Can I really build it?

      Every model is checked by computer: all pieces are standard parts in real colours, every piece is attached to the base (0 floating), no two pieces overlap (0 collisions), joints are staggered like a brick wall, and the centre of mass sits over the base. The site shows these checks for your sign and says so plainly if one fails. Models are not physically build-tested, so treat your first build as a test. Brand colours are matched to the nearest of 37 common brick colours, so some shades will shift.

      Can I use any company’s logo?

      Logos are trademarks of their owners. Building one for your own desk, or for your own company, is generally fine. Selling kits or signs of someone else’s logo, or using one in a way that suggests they endorse you, is not: ask the owner first. This isn’t legal advice.

      Is this official?

      No. Logo to Bricks is a free, open-source, unofficial fan project, not affiliated with, sponsored or endorsed by the LEGO Group or BrickLink. It sells nothing and earns nothing: no affiliate links, no commission, no tracking.
